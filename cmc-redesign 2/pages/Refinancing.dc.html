Refinancing a Cayman mortgage: when a new rate pays for itself

A lower rate is only half the sum. Moving a mortgage in Cayman means new stamp duty on the charge, legal and valuation fees and possibly an early-repayment charge, so the question is how many months of savings it takes to earn those back.

When refinancing makes sense

Most Cayman mortgages are variable and priced off KYD prime, so when prime moves your rate moves with it; a cut you get automatically is not a reason to refinance. Refinancing is worth a look when one of these is true:

  • Another lender's margin over prime is lower than yours. Prime is the same everywhere; the margin is what differs. Half a percent on a CI$400,000 balance is about CI$2,000 a year.
  • Your circumstances changed and your lender will not re-price: a better salary, a cleared loan, or equity that has grown past the lender's loan-to-value threshold.
  • You want a different term. Shortening it cuts total interest; lengthening it lowers the payment. Your own bank may do this as a variation without a full refinance.
  • You need to borrow against equity for a renovation, land or a second property. See the equity calculator for how much is available at your lender's cap.

It rarely makes sense when the rate gap is small and little of the term is left; the interest you would save is front-loaded in the early years, and the fees are the same whenever you pay them.

Same lender or a new one?

Ask your own bank first
  • A re-price or variation can avoid new stamp duty and most legal cost
  • Bring a competing offer in writing; it is the strongest argument
  • Ask for the margin over prime, not just "the rate"
Move if
  • The new margin covers the fees within a few years and you plan to stay
  • Your bank will not match, or will not lend the extra you need
  • The new lender waives or reduces its arrangement fee

Step by step

  1. Read your current offer letter for the margin over prime, the early-repayment terms and the remaining term.
  2. Get two written offers, one from your bank and one from a competitor, each stating margin, fees and any cash-back.
  3. Ask an attorney for a fixed quote and whether your case is a new charge or a variation (this decides the stamp duty).
  4. Run the break-even with the real numbers. If you will be in the home past the break-even month, it pays.
  5. Keep the payment the same if you can afford it: paying the old amount at the new rate shortens the term and multiplies the saving.

Common questions

Do I pay stamp duty again when I refinance?

If a new legal charge is registered, which is what happens when you move lenders, yes: at 1% or 1.5% of the sum secured under the Stamp Duty Act's mortgage schedule. If your existing lender varies the current charge, duty is generally only charged on any increase in the sum secured. Your attorney confirms which applies.

Can I refinance to take cash out?

Usually, up to the lender's loan-to-value cap and subject to affordability. The equity calculator shows the ceiling; the whole new sum is what gets stamped.

Will a rate cut from the bank change my payment automatically?

On a prime-linked variable mortgage, yes: the bank re-sets the payment when prime changes, with notice. That is not a refinance and costs nothing.

Is there a Government concession for refinancing?

No. The Caymanian concessions apply to stamp duty on the purchase (the transfer), not to the mortgage charge.

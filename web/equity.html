Banks set their own rules and will not always go to these ratios. A higher ratio also means a larger loan, and the payment still has to pass the bank's affordability test.

Where the suggested ratios come from

  • Caymanians and long-term residents, up to 90%. Property Cayman's mortgage guide puts primary-residence financing at 85–90%, with 10–15% down. Cayman National advertises a 95% home loan, and Butterfield's own page states up to 85% on a residential mortgage — so 90% sits in the middle of what local lenders publish, and a first-time Caymanian buyer may do better.
  • Residents, up to 80%. Butterfield told Property Cayman its "lending policy is the same for Caymanians, PR Status holders, RERC and work permit holders", so a resident may well be offered more than 80% at some banks. It is set lower here because not every lender treats the two groups alike.
  • Non-residents, up to 70%. ERA Cayman's non-resident guide says financing is available "up to about 70 percent of the property value, for terms up to 20 years". Crighton Properties puts non-resident deposits at 20–30%. Some banks are stricter still, at 65% or less.
  • Other property types are lower. Butterfield publishes up to 70% on raw land and up to 65% on investment property, over shorter terms.

Worth knowing

  • Releasing equity is new borrowing. It carries mortgage stamp duty on the new charge, and the payment must pass the bank's affordability test — roughly a third of gross income is the usual guide.
  • The bank lends against its own valuation. An estimate from a listing site or a neighbour's sale price is not what the decision rests on.
  • Equity is not cash until you borrow against it or sell, and selling costs an agent's commission and legal fees.
  • Rates here are usually the Cayman prime rate plus a margin, so a payment worked out today can rise.

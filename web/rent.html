Buying Renting and investing the difference

Buying counts the property's value less what you still owe and what it costs to sell, plus anything you invested when the rent was higher than the mortgage. Renting counts the deposit and fees you did not spend, invested, plus whatever you saved each month buying would have cost you.

What this counts

  • Buying: the deposit, stamp duty on the transfer and the mortgage, registry, legal and bank fees, the first year's insurance, then the mortgage, strata, insurance and upkeep every month.
  • Renting: the rent, rising each year, and nothing else. No duty, no strata, no roof.
  • Both paths invest whatever the other one is spending, so the comparison is like for like.

What it cannot know

  • Whether Cayman property keeps rising at the rate you typed. The last few years are not a promise.
  • Whether you would really invest the difference every month, which is what makes renting competitive.
  • Hurricane damage, a special strata assessment, a rent increase larger than you planned for, or having to move for work.

Worth knowing

  • There is no annual property tax in the Cayman Islands, which is why the one-off costs dominate.
  • Stamp duty is the largest single cost of buying, and the concessions for Caymanian buyers change the answer considerably — set your status on the left.
  • Selling costs matter: they are why buying rarely pays off over two or three years.
